# Regional Reporting, Not a Ranking

A region with more reported cybercrime may have more crime, more reporting, a broader definition, or a larger population. These figures are shares and case lists inside stated datasets. They are not a ranking of countries, and no region is described here as a centre of crime.

Every figure below is single-source. None of them is a victim census.

## A U.S. reporting channel

The FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center recorded 1,008,597 complaints and US$20.877 billion in reported losses in 2025, a 26 percent increase on 2024. Investment fraud was US$8.65 billion, and business email compromise was part of the total. Single-source.

That is a U.S. reporting channel. It is not a global census, and it is not a denominator for Africa or any other region.

## Europe

ENISA's Threat Landscape 2025 summary gives a base of 4,875 EU incidents. Inside that set, DDoS was 77 percent, phishing was 60 percent of access paths, and vulnerability exploitation was 21.3 percent of intrusion access points. The categories overlap. They are not a pie that sums to 100, and they do not say which country was "most attacked." Single-source.

Read the denominator before the percentage.

## Oceania, and a gap

One regional row records 87,400 or more cybercrime reports in FY23–24 and 42,500 or more hotline calls in FY24–25, alongside a data gap for small Pacific island nations. Reports are not victims. Hotline calls are not the same thing as reports. A total that omits small states is not a complete regional picture. Single-source.

## Southeast Asia and a sample

Documented Predator and Pegasus deployments in Southeast Asia sit in the same case set as Operation Triangulation and LightSpy implants. That is a set of cases, not a prevalence estimate, and not a claim that every device in the region is compromised. Single-source.

Operation Triangulation was an iMessage zero-click chain affecting iOS before 16.6.1. The patch boundary used here is iOS 16.6.1. Single-source.

A 15-device sample can show that a technique was observed. It cannot show that the technique is common, or that a country is the most targeted. The selection method is not re-derived here.

## An enforcement action and a decree

On 22 October 2026, reporting described a UAE-led Operation Confrontation Ghost, run with Interpol and partners in 30 countries, that produced arrests and seizures. This page does not repeat an arrest or seizure total. The operation is an enforcement action. It is not evidence that a country is uniquely unsafe, and it is not a basis for naming a client. Single-source.

UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 was published in 2021 and came into force on 2 January 2022. It criminalises online content that defames persons or entities, and related conduct. Whether it applies to any particular publication is a legal question. This page does not decide it. Single-source.

A documented targeting case, including the Million Dollar Dissident episode, is one case. It is not a prevalence study, and no victim count is added here. Single-source.

## Where the file is thin

The Africa file in this ledger does not support a prevalence claim. One operation figure was rejected because the linked source did not support the number as stated. A claim that most cases in the region are AI-assisted is unverified. Neither is used. The absence of a reliable number is not the absence of harm, and the U.S. complaint total above is not African data.

The Latin America file does not support a ranking or a published family list. That material is unverified here, so it is not stated. A banking-trojan threat can be real in a region without a league table. No prevalence and no victim count are given.

## What this page does not say

It does not name spyware clients. It does not rank countries or malware families. It does not turn a sample, a share, or a reporting channel into a map of danger.
