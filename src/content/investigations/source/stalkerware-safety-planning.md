# Stalkerware and Safety Planning

Stalkerware is usually someone who already has access: a partner, a former partner, or a family member who knows the device, the accounts, and the routines. That changes the advice. "Just reset the phone" can destroy evidence. "Just leave" can be dangerous. This page does not explain how to install or hide tracking software.

No prevalence figure is used. A rejected count is not repeated, because no reliable prevalence figure is established here.

## Preserve evidence first

A factory reset can destroy the artefacts that would show what happened. Document what you can, get advice, and then decide what to reset and when. Single-source.

The first call is to a trusted advocate: a domestic-violence service, a lawyer, or a trained safety planner. Do not confront the person with a technical accusation. Safety first, evidence second.

## Tracking is not only an app

A phone can be followed through an account, a shared plan, location sharing, or a Bluetooth device that advertises. Research on Bluetooth Low Energy advertising shows that a rotating address does not, by itself, stop tracking when service data and timing stay stable. That is a mechanism, not a count of how often it happens. Single-source.

Apple and Google added cross-platform unwanted-tracking alerts in May 2024. The alerts depend on tracker compatibility and on the tracker being separated from its owner. An alert is a prompt to get advice. It does not prove who placed a device, and it is not a guarantee. Single-source.

Check accounts and location sharing from a device the other person cannot access, if that is possible. A new phone and new accounts are a decision to make with an advocate, not a universal first step.

Do not disable accessibility tools you rely on. Audit who has access. Do not remove your own support.

## What this does not mean

It does not mean every partner is a stalker, or that every tracker alert is an emergency. It does not mean the person being targeted is responsible. It means the response should be safety-led and guided by someone trained to help.
