# Evidence Method

A verification project is defined by what it refuses to publish as well as by what it prints. This page is about that process. It does not reproduce excluded material.

## Four different states

**Rejected.** Two claims were rejected and are not used as examples. One was an airport Wi-Fi story whose wording did not match the record. One was an operation figure whose linked source did not support the number as stated. A rejected claim stays in the ledger with its correction trail. It is not available for prose, and the corrected details are not slipped in here either.

**Unverified.** An Android baseband claim remains unverified. There is no primary baseband source in this pass. Unverified is not the same as false. The claim stays out of the articles until a primary source is added.

**Suppressed.** One source list was incomplete against its own citations. References that list does not define are not used, and they are not reproduced on this page. That is a citation rule, not a finding about the underlying event.

**Legally held, then scoped.** A UAE-led enforcement action and a UAE decree are confirmed at the source level and are described on the [regional reporting](regional-reporting-limits) page. This investigation does not name clients and does not draw a legal conclusion about the decree.

The reasons differ. The ledger keeps them separate.

## How a sentence gets in

1. The claim comes first. A sentence without a ledger row does not ship.
2. One verdict per claim: confirmed, adjusted, rejected, or unverified.
3. An adjusted claim keeps the old wording, the new wording, and the reason.
4. Undefined references do not ship.
5. Unverified is a complete answer. Repetition does not promote it.
6. Legal and sensitivity review sit at the publication gate. A draft can be revised. Publishing cannot.

## The counts, as derived

The derived ledger has 201 claims: 85 confirmed, 95 adjusted, 6 rejected, and 15 pending. Of the 201, 186 were checked. The pending count is 14 pending claims plus one named duplicate.

The 85 confirmed claims are not one path. 39 were verified in the event ledger. 46 were imported and carry an earlier status. Import is not the same thing as that event-by-event check.

An older frame used 79 units: 48 verified, 19 plausible, 10 contradicted, and 2 outdated. That frame is historical. It is not the current claim count. In the crosswalk, 33 of those "verified" units became confirmed, 7 became adjusted, and 2 became rejected. Six "plausible" units became confirmed, and 13 stayed unverified. Four units split across more than one claim. One unit was superseded. Two units, V3A-19 and V3A-20, are named omissions outside the 201.

135 claims have no verification unit. That includes an older "125 held" set plus later imports. The old 125 is not the current pending count. Current pending is 15.

V3A-19 and V3A-20 are named here so the ledger does not look more complete than it is. They are not entered as new claims on this page. Adding those rows before this omission was named would have been circular, and they are not added now.

These counts are derived from the ledger. They are not a census of all cybercrime, and they are not a second verification of every imported claim.
