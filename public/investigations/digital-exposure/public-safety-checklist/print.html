Public Safety Checklist

A practical guide based on the investigation. It is deliberately short. A list of a hundred items is a way of saying "this is not your problem"; these are the controls that change outcomes.

The 20-minute baseline (everyone)

Do these once, then revisit quarterly.

  • ☐ Turn on automatic updates for phone, computer, browser, and apps.
  • ☐ Install a password manager and change the passwords on your five most important accounts.
  • ☐ Add a passkey or hardware security key to your primary email account.
  • ☐ Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere it is offered; prefer an app or key over SMS.
  • ☐ Review active sessions on email, cloud, and social accounts; sign out unfamiliar devices.
  • ☐ Check recovery settings — recovery email, phone number, trusted devices, recovery contacts.
  • ☐ Audit app permissions — location, photos, microphone, contacts, accessibility, notifications.
  • ☐ Remove apps and browser extensions you do not use.
  • ☐ Enable encrypted backups (phone and computer) and test that you can restore.
  • ☐ Write down your recovery codes and store them offline.

Travellers

Before you go

  • ☐ Confirm the official hotel/booking app and enable 2FA on the booking account.
  • ☐ Turn off auto-join for open Wi-Fi networks.
  • ☐ Set up a VPN you trust, or plan to use cellular data for sensitive tasks.
  • ☐ Remove sensitive files you do not need for the trip.
  • ☐ Enable "find my device" and note your device IDs.

On arrival

  • ☐ Confirm the exact network name at reception.
  • ☐ Never enter an email/social password on a captive portal.
  • ☐ Never install a certificate or configuration profile to get online.
  • ☐ Check the portal's domain before typing anything.
  • ☐ Use the deadbolt; report lost keycards immediately.

While there

  • ☐ Do banking, email recovery, and password changes on cellular data or a VPN.
  • ☐ Keep Bluetooth off when not using accessories.
  • ☐ Turn off AirDrop/file sharing to "Everyone".
  • ☐ Treat booking messages with links as hostile; verify in the app.
  • ☐ Do not leave devices unattended in public areas.

After

  • ☐ Forget the network.
  • ☐ Review active sessions and OAuth grants.
  • ☐ Check bank and card statements.
  • ☐ If anything felt wrong, change the affected password from a different device.

iPhone users

  • ☐ Update iOS as soon as a security update appears.
  • ☐ Use a strong alphanumeric passcode, not six digits.
  • ☐ Enable Stolen Device Protection (where available).
  • ☐ Turn off lock-screen message previews.
  • ☐ Review Settings → General → VPN & Device Management for unknown profiles.
  • ☐ Review Settings → Privacy & Security → App Tracking / Analytics.
  • ☐ If you are high-risk: enable Lockdown Mode.
  • ☐ Register a hardware key for your Apple account.
  • ☐ Review trusted devices and recovery contacts twice a year.

Android users

  • ☐ Update Android and Play system updates; check the security patch level.
  • ☐ Keep Google Play Protect on.
  • ☐ Disable "install unknown apps" for browsers and messaging apps.
  • ☐ Audit Settings → Accessibility → Installed services monthly.
  • ☐ Audit Device admin apps and Display over other apps.
  • ☐ Use the photo picker instead of granting full library access.
  • ☐ Consider Advanced Protection if you are high-risk.
  • ☐ Do not install APKs from messages, forums, or "mod" sites.

Families

  • ☐ Agree a family code word for urgent money requests; verify by voice on a known number.
  • ☐ Turn on parental controls via the platform, not a random app.
  • ☐ Teach children that a person asking for codes or photos is never legitimate.
  • ☐ Set purchase approvals and disable in-app purchases where unnecessary.
  • ☐ Review privacy settings on games and social apps; disable public location sharing.
  • ☐ Agree what to do if a device is lost or an account is taken over.
  • ☐ Keep an offline list of recovery codes for the family accounts.
  • ☐ Discuss sextortion: do not pay, do not send more, preserve evidence, report.

Cryptocurrency holders

  • ☐ Use a hardware wallet for long-term holdings.
  • ☐ Store the seed phrase offline, never photographed, never in cloud notes.
  • ☐ Verify addresses on the hardware screen, not the computer.
  • ☐ Keep a separate hot wallet with limited funds for new protocols.
  • ☐ Revoke approvals regularly.
  • ☐ Use hardware-key 2FA on exchanges and email; remove SMS where possible.
  • ☐ Enable withdrawal allowlists.
  • ☐ Send a small test transaction before large transfers.
  • ☐ Treat all unsolicited support as fraud.
  • ☐ Do not publicly disclose holdings.
  • ☐ Make an inheritance plan that does not put the seed in one place.

If you suspect a compromise

Do

  • ☐ Disconnect the device from networks if you suspect active malware.
  • ☐ Change passwords from a different, trusted device, starting with email.
  • ☐ Revoke sessions and remove unknown devices.
  • ☐ Check recovery settings and OAuth grants.
  • ☐ Contact your bank from a different phone.
  • ☐ Preserve logs and the device if you may need forensic help.
  • ☐ Report to the relevant national fraud/cybercrime centre.

Do not

  • ☐ Do not factory reset a device you intend to have examined.
  • ☐ Do not delete messages or accounts to "clean up".
  • ☐ Do not install "spyware detector" apps from ads.
  • ☐ Do not confront a suspected attacker using the compromised device.
  • ☐ Do not pay an extortion demand without specialist advice.
  • ☐ Do not assume a password change is sufficient — sessions and tokens persist.

If you received an Apple Threat Notification

  • ☐ Treat it as a high-confidence indicator of targeting.
  • ☐ Do not reset the device.
  • ☐ Contact a recognised digital-security helpline or forensic provider.
  • ☐ Enable Lockdown Mode after consultation.
  • ☐ Inform close contacts through a separate channel.
  • ☐ Preserve the device and its logs.

If your phone number is ported or loses service

  • ☐ Contact the carrier from another line; request a port freeze.
  • ☐ Change email password first, then financial accounts.
  • ☐ Revoke sessions; remove unknown devices.
  • ☐ Check for new recovery emails/phone numbers added by the attacker.
  • ☐ Notify your bank and monitor accounts.
  • ☐ Warn contacts about impersonation.

If you run a hotel or small property

  • ☐ Separate guest, corporate, and device networks.
  • ☐ Enable client isolation on guest Wi-Fi.
  • ☐ Replace default credentials on all network and lock-management interfaces.
  • ☐ Patch access points and controllers on a schedule, not at refurbishment.
  • ☐ Require MFA for remote access by IT contractors.
  • ☐ Log and review administrator activity.
  • ☐ Enforce keycard revocation at checkout and on loss.
  • ☐ Train front desk staff on identity verification for account changes.
  • ☐ Have a breach response plan that includes guest notification.

A note on trade-offs

Every control here costs something: convenience, money, time, or access to a feature. That is normal. Security is not a state of being protected; it is a set of choices about which risks you are willing to carry.

The goal is not to eliminate risk. It is to make sure the risks you carry are the ones you chose.
